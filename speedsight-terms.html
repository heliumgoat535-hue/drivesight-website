1. What SpeedSight does

SpeedSight measures your speed with your phone's GPS and shows it as a speedometer, a HUD view for the windshield, a picture-in-picture window and a floating speedometer over other apps. It saves your trips on your phone.

The free version includes the speedometer, HUD, picture-in-picture and your 3 newest trips, with banner ads on the Trips and Settings screens. SpeedSight Pro adds posted speed limits where map data has them, over-limit alerts, speed camera warnings, the floating speedometer, your full trip history, GPX export and colour themes, and removes the ads.

5. What the app can and cannot tell you

Speed. GPS speed is usually accurate, but it can be wrong or late in tunnels, between tall buildings, under heavy tree cover, when the phone has a weak signal, or right after the signal comes back. It is not a calibrated instrument and is not proof of your speed.

Speed limits. Posted limits come from OpenStreetMap, a map made by volunteers. Many roads have no limit in the data, and some limits are out of date or wrong. Temporary limits, roadworks, school zones and variable signs are usually missing. The road signs always apply, whatever the app shows.

Speed cameras. Camera locations come from OpenStreetMap and are incomplete. Cameras are added, moved and removed without notice, and mobile cameras are not included. A missing warning does not mean there is no camera.

Where camera warnings are illegal. Some countries ban speed camera warnings for drivers. SpeedSight turns them off when your phone's country is France, Germany or Switzerland. If you drive somewhere else that bans them, it is your responsibility to turn camera warnings off in Settings.

Alerts are a guide to help you keep track of your speed. Don't rely on them alone.
