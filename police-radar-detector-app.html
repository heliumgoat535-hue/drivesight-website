Police Radar Detector App for Android: What You Actually Get

September 18, 2026 · 9 min read

Cyberlab Automation Android developer · Builder of DriveSight

A police radar detector app does not detect radar. No phone has a receiver for the 24 and 34 GHz bands a radar gun uses, so that part is settled and we covered why in do radar detector apps work. What the category does instead is warn you about enforcement that can be mapped or that announces itself, which turns out to cover most of the ways drivers actually get caught.

This page is the practical side: what the four alert types are, which parts are genuinely free, and the Android-specific details that decide whether any of it reaches you while you drive. Those details matter more than the app you pick, and almost nobody mentions them.

The four alert types, and how much to trust each

Alert typeWhere the data comes fromHow reliable
Fixed speed & red light camerasPublic records, mapped onceHighest. They never move
ALPR / Flock plate readersCommunity-maintained map layersHigh, with operator and facing direction
Patrol car reportsOther drivers, right nowOnly as fresh as the last car through
Police aircraftPublic ADS-B broadcastsObjective. The aircraft reports itself

The ordering matters when you are judging an app. A large camera database is table stakes and easy to verify. Live reports are the part that varies wildly between apps, because they depend on how many drivers use it near you. Aircraft coverage is rare enough that most apps in this category simply do not have it.

Mapped cameras are the most dependable layer, because a camera bolted to a gantry is in the same place tomorrow

What is free, and what is not

“Free” is the most common thing people add to this search, so here is the honest shape of it across the category rather than a pitch.

Free almost everywhere: dash cam recording, browsing the map yourself, and basic proximity alerts. These cost the developer nothing per user beyond hosting, so there is no reason to charge.

Usually paid: a large maintained camera database, live plate reader data, aircraft tracking, and cloud backup. These have a recurring cost, whether that is data licensing, server time or the work of keeping a database current. In DriveSight the recording and the map are free, and the police and camera alert layers are part of Pro.

A quick warning about “completely free” apps

If an app offers unlimited live enforcement data with no subscription and no ads, ask how it is funded. Sometimes the answer is fine, such as a hobby project on public data. Sometimes the answer is your location history being sold on. Data that costs money to maintain is paid for somehow, and it is worth knowing which way.

The Android details that decide whether it works at all

This is the part that sinks more installs than any missing feature. An alert app that gets suspended by the operating system is worse than no app, because you trust it and it stays silent.

Background execution and battery optimisation

Android suspends background apps aggressively, and Samsung, Xiaomi and OnePlus skins are more aggressive still. An alerting app has to run as a foreground service with a persistent notification to survive, and you almost always have to exempt it from battery optimisation by hand in Settings.

If an app alerts fine for five minutes and then goes quiet on the mount, this is the reason nine times out of ten. It is the first thing to check before blaming the app's data.

Battery draw, realistically

Alerting on its own is cheap. It reuses a GPS fix the phone already has and polls data on a timer. Recording video at the same time is the expensive part, and together with screen-on time it will usually outrun a weak USB socket.

Use a real car charger or a hardwired supply. On a low-output port you will watch the battery fall during a drive even while plugged in, which people routinely misread as a bug in the app.

Android Auto

Android Auto limits what any app may display while the car is moving, so a head unit version is necessarily simpler than the phone screen: a map and alerts, not the full interface. That is a platform rule rather than a shortcut by the developer.

The head unit view is deliberately simplified. We covered the full setup in the Android Auto guide.

Mounting and the things that quietly matter

A phone doing this job needs a clear view of the sky for GPS, airflow so it does not thermally throttle in summer, and a position where glancing at it is not a hazard. A windscreen mount high and to the side generally beats a dashboard cradle baking in direct sun. If the app also records, the camera needs a clean line through the glass, away from the wiper dead zone.

Is there one for iPhone?

There are alert apps on iOS, but DriveSight is Android only, so this guide is written for Android and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. iOS restricts sustained background location and camera access more tightly, which is a large part of why continuous recording combined with live alerting is more common on Android. The radar point is identical on both: neither platform has a phone with a radar receiver in it.

Do you still need a hardware radar detector?

They are not really competitors, because they cover different threats. A hardware detector catches an instant-on radar or laser gun already aimed at your car, which no app will ever do. An app catches fixed cameras, plate readers, reported patrol cars and aircraft, none of which a detector can see because none of them transmit at you.

If most of your risk is cameras and aerial enforcement, the app covers more ground. If you drive long highway miles where troopers run instant-on radar, hardware still earns its place, and plenty of drivers run both. Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a free police radar detector app for Android?

Yes, with a split. Recording, browsing the map and basic alerts are usually free because they cost nothing per user. A large maintained camera database, live plate reader data and aircraft tracking normally sit behind a subscription, because that data has an ongoing cost. Be sceptical of unlimited live data with no revenue model at all.

Will it keep working with the screen off?

Only as a foreground service with a persistent notification, and only if you exempt it from battery optimisation. Android suspends background apps aggressively and manufacturer skins are worse. An app that goes quiet after a few minutes is nearly always a battery optimisation problem rather than a data problem.

Is there a police radar detector app for iPhone?

There are alert apps on iOS, but DriveSight is Android only. iOS limits sustained background location and camera use more tightly, which is why continuous recording plus live alerting is more common on Android. No app on either platform detects radar.

Do you still need a hardware radar detector if you have an app?

They cover different threats. Hardware catches instant-on radar and laser aimed at you. An app catches cameras, plate readers, reported patrol cars and aircraft, which transmit nothing for hardware to hear. Many drivers run both; if cameras are your main risk, the app does more.

How much battery does it use?

Alerting alone is light, since it reuses an existing GPS fix and polls on a timer. Recording video is the expensive part and, with the screen on, will outpace a weak charger. Use a proper car charger or hardwired power and expect a slow drain rather than a gain while recording.

Does it work on Android Auto?

Some apps do, including DriveSight, but Android Auto restricts what can be shown while driving. The head unit gets a simplified map and alert layer while the detailed view stays on the phone. That is a platform rule, not a limitation the developer chose.
